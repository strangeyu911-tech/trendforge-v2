Nearly four in five professional developers now use AI coding assistants at least once a week, according to the 2026 Developer Pulse survey released Friday by London-based research firm DevMetrics.

The survey of 24,000 developers across 31 countries found that 78% use tools such as GitHub Copilot, Cursor, or Claude Code weekly, up from 54% in last year's edition. Respondents estimated that 41% of the code they committed in the past month was AI-generated or AI-assisted, though only 12% said they merge AI output without manual review.

"The debate has shifted from adoption to accountability," said Daniel Okafor, lead author of the report. "Teams are now arguing about review standards for machine-written code, not whether to allow it."

Productivity perceptions were mixed. Developers reported saving a median of 6.5 hours per week, but 34% said debugging AI-generated code had introduced new classes of subtle bugs, particularly in concurrency and edge-case handling. Enterprise respondents named security scanning of AI output as their top investment priority for the second half of 2026.

The survey also found that Cursor grew its paying user base to 1.9 million, while GitHub said Copilot now serves 15 million users, cementing AI pair programming as a default layer of the software toolchain.
