US job postings mentioning AI skills rose 34% year over year in the second quarter, even as entry-level software engineering listings fell 19%, according to data published Thursday by the Hiring Lab at job site Indeed.

The bifurcation is reshaping tech hiring. Postings for "AI engineer," "agent operations," and "ML platform" roles climbed to 8.7% of all tech listings, while junior front-end and QA positions contracted for the fifth consecutive quarter. Average advertised salaries for AI-labeled roles reached $178,000, a 31% premium over general software roles.

"Companies are not hiring fewer engineers overall — they are hiring different ones," said Laura Benton, chief economist at the Hiring Lab. "The bar for entry-level applicants now includes supervising AI output, which many new graduates were never trained to do."

A parallel survey by payroll provider Gusto found that 27% of mid-size firms have frozen back-office headcount growth explicitly citing AI automation, most commonly in payroll processing and customer support.

The trend is spilling into education: coding bootcamp enrollments fell 41% from their 2023 peak, while university certificate programs in "AI-augmented software development" reported a threefold increase in applicants for the fall semester, according to trade group CIRR.
