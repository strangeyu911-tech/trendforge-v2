Nvidia on Tuesday took the wraps off Rubin Ultra, its next-generation data-center GPU, claiming a 3.6-fold improvement in training throughput over the Blackwell B200 for large mixture-of-experts models.

Built on TSMC's N3P process and paired with 288 GB of HBM4 memory, Rubin Ultra delivers 15 petaflops of dense FP4 compute per package, Nvidia said. Volume shipments are slated for the second quarter of 2027, with hyperscaler allocations "already spoken for through 2028," according to chief financial officer Colette Kress.

Nvidia also reported preliminary quarterly data-center revenue of $68 billion, up 92% year over year, with networking now contributing 19% of segment sales as customers build ever-larger NVLink domains. The Vera Rubin NVL144 rack configuration connects 144 GPUs with 260 terabytes per second of aggregate bandwidth.

"Rubin is designed for the agentic era, where inference demand dwarfs training," said chief executive Jensen Huang during the keynote, noting that inference now accounts for 64% of deployed Blackwell capacity.

Analyst Patrick Moorhead of Moor Insights said the announcement "resets the goalposts" for rivals: AMD's MI500 and Google's TPU v7 are both expected later this year, but neither matches Rubin's memory bandwidth of 20.5 terabytes per second per GPU.
