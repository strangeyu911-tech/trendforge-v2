Artificial intelligence will displace 92 million jobs globally by 2030 while creating 170 million new ones, a net gain of 78 million positions, the Global Labor Futures Institute said in its annual Workforce Transitions report published Thursday.

The London-based body found that 12% of existing roles across the 42 economies studied have already been "substantially redesigned" around AI tools, up from 5% in 2024. Clerical data entry, basic bookkeeping, and tier-one customer support face the steepest declines, with projected headcount reductions of 34%, 28%, and 26% respectively.

"The displacement is real but concentrated; the creation is diffuse and slower to appear in statistics," said Ingrid Halvorsen, the institute's director of research. She noted that the fastest-growing new categories — AI workflow auditors, agent operations managers, and synthetic-data curators — "barely existed in job boards three years ago."

The report estimates that 39% of core worker skills will change by 2030, and that employers plan to retrain an average of 22% of their workforce over the next three years. Wage data show a 14% premium for roles explicitly requiring AI orchestration skills.

Halvorsen urged governments to expand portable training accounts, warning that "the net figure hides severe local pain" in administrative-service hubs.
