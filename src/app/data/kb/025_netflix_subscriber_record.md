Netflix reported Wednesday that global paid memberships reached 382 million in the second quarter, beating Wall Street estimates, as its advertising-supported plan accounted for 45% of new sign-ups in markets where it is offered.

Revenue rose 16% year over year to $11.4 billion, with the ad business contributing $1.3 billion in the quarter — triple its level two years ago. Average revenue per member in the US and Canada climbed to $18.20 following January's price adjustment.

"Our ads tier now delivers reach that rivals broadcast networks in twelve countries," said Elena Marsh, Netflix's chief financial officer, on the earnings call. She said engagement per member hit a record 2.4 hours daily, helped by live events: the platform's boxing card in June drew 41 million global viewers, its largest live audience to date.

Content spending for 2026 is guided at $19 billion. The streamer's Korean and Japanese slates drove a quarter of viewing hours outside English-language programming, led by the thriller series "Ashes of Seoul," which logged 190 million viewing hours in 28 days.

Netflix raised its full-year margin target to 31% and announced a further $15 billion share buyback. Shares gained 5.8% in after-hours trading.
