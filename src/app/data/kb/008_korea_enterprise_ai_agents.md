South Korea's largest conglomerates are moving AI agents from pilot projects into core operations, pushing the domestic enterprise agent market past $2.1 billion in the first half of 2026, according to data released Sunday by the Korea Software Industry Association.

The association's survey of 480 large firms found that 47% are now running at least one production agent system, up from 18% a year ago. Samsung SDS said its agent platform Brity Works now handles 31% of internal IT support tickets without human intervention, while Naver reported its HyperCLOVA-based agents process 1.2 million customer inquiries daily for retail clients.

"Korean chaebol treat agent deployment as a productivity race," said Park Ji-hoon, a professor of AI policy at KAIST. "The government target of 30% labor-productivity gains in services by 2030 is driving board-level urgency."

Financial services lead adoption: Shinhan Bank said its loan-review agents cut processing time for small-business credit from five days to six hours, and KB Securities deploys agents that draft 78% of routine compliance filings.

The market is attracting global vendors — OpenAI opened a Seoul office in May — but local players hold a 61% share, helped by Korean-language fine-tuning and strict on-premises data requirements in finance and the public sector.
