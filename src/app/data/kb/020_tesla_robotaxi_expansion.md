Tesla said Sunday its robotaxi service now operates in 12 US cities and completes roughly 35,000 paid rides per day, a tenfold increase since the Austin pilot began in January.

The expansion added Phoenix, Miami, Atlanta, and five other metro areas this month. In Austin, regulators approved Tesla's request to remove safety drivers from 60% of its fleet in July after the company logged 4.8 million autonomous miles with a disengagement rate of 0.31 per 10,000 miles, according to filings with Texas authorities.

"The economics finally work: our cost per mile is $0.87, against $1.42 for a human-driven ride-hail trip," said Rosa Delgado, Tesla's vice president of autonomy, on a media call. Average fare per ride is $9.40, and fleet utilization stands at 6.1 rides per vehicle per hour during peak periods.

Tesla's robotaxi fleet numbers about 9,000 vehicles, all Model Y units running Hardware 5. The company plans to add 20,000 purpose-built Cybercab units in the first half of 2027 pending federal exemption approvals.

Analysts at Morgan Stanley estimate the service contributed $210 million in second-quarter revenue — under 1% of Tesla's total, but growing 40% month over month. Rival Waymo operates in 9 cities with about 28,000 daily rides.
