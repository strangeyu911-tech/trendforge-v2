OpenAI on Monday unveiled AgentOS, an enterprise platform that lets companies deploy and govern fleets of autonomous AI agents, marking its most aggressive push yet into corporate software.

The San Francisco-based company said more than 500 enterprises, including logistics giant Maersk and insurer Prudential, participated in a six-month pilot program. OpenAI claims pilot customers automated an average of 38% of repetitive back-office tasks within the first quarter of deployment.

"Enterprises no longer ask whether agents work; they ask how fast they can scale them," said Sarah Linden, OpenAI's chief operating officer, at the launch event in San Francisco. She disclosed that the company's annualized enterprise revenue has reached $4.2 billion, up from $1.6 billion a year earlier.

AgentOS includes an audit trail system that logs every agent action, a feature analysts say addresses compliance concerns in finance and healthcare. Pricing starts at $60 per agent seat per month, undercutting rival offerings from Microsoft and Salesforce by roughly 20%, according to data from consultancy Gartner.

Industry analyst Priya Raman of Forrester noted that OpenAI now counts 62% of the Fortune 100 as paying customers, adding that agent governance, not model quality, has become "the decisive battleground" of 2026.
