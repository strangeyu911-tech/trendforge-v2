Apple has confirmed it will unveil its first foldable iPhone at its September 9 event, people familiar with the plans said, with pricing starting at $1,999 and pre-orders opening the following Friday.

The device, internally dubbed iPhone Fold, features a 7.8-inch crease-minimized inner display supplied by Samsung Display and a 5.5-inch cover screen, according to supply-chain documents. Apple has booked initial panel capacity for 12 million units in the first year, the documents show.

"Apple waited until the hinge and battery trade-offs disappeared," said Evelyn Chung, senior analyst at Meridian Research. "Our teardown estimates suggest the crease depth is under 8 microns — effectively invisible." Chung forecasts foldables will reach 9% of global smartphone revenue in 2027, up from 3.4% this year.

The Fold will ship with a new multitasking interface that lets three apps run side by side, and a titanium-carbon hinge rated for 600,000 folds, roughly triple the industry standard. Battery capacity is 5,100 mAh, split across two cells.

Suppliers Luxshare and Goertek have both added assembly lines in Vietnam for the launch. Apple shares rose 2.3% on the report, adding about $74 billion in market value.
