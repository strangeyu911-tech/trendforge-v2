The Tokyo International Robot Exhibition opened Wednesday with a record 640 exhibitors, as Japan's worsening labor shortage pushes service robots from novelty to necessity across hotels, hospitals, and convenience stores.

Organizers from the Japan Robot Association expect 145,000 visitors over the four-day show, up 22% from the previous edition. The domestic service-robot market is on pace to reach 1.2 trillion yen ($8.1 billion) this fiscal year, the association said, with eldercare and logistics the fastest-growing segments at 38% and 31% growth respectively.

"Japan's working-age population shrinks by about 600,000 people a year; robots are the only staffing plan many operators have left," said Yuki Tanaka, the association's secretary general, at the opening ceremony.

Highlights included Fanuc's new cobot line for small factories, priced 40% below its 2024 models, and a nursing-assist robot from startup Telexistence that completed a 90-day trial across 12 Tokyo care homes, lifting an average of 55 patient transfers per day.

Government data show robot density in Japanese manufacturing reached 419 units per 10,000 workers, the world's highest, but service-sector adoption trails South Korea and China. Subsidies covering half of purchase costs for small businesses took effect in April.
