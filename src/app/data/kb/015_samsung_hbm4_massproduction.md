Samsung Electronics said Monday it has begun mass production of 12-layer HBM4 memory at its Pyeongtaek complex, becoming the second supplier qualified for Nvidia's next-generation Rubin platform after SK Hynix.

The new stacks deliver 2 terabytes per second of bandwidth each, a 60% improvement over HBM3E, with power consumption reduced by 22% through a new thermal compression bonding process. Samsung said monthly capacity will reach 170,000 wafers by year-end.

"Qualification for Rubin validates two years of process redesign," said Kim Jae-won, head of Samsung's memory division, in a statement. Industry sources estimate Samsung secured roughly 30% of Nvidia's initial HBM4 allocation, with SK Hynix retaining about 55% and Micron the remainder.

The high-bandwidth memory market is projected to reach $54 billion in 2026, up 74% year over year, driven by AI accelerator demand, according to TrendForce. HBM4 commands prices 1.8 times higher than HBM3E on a per-gigabyte basis.

Samsung's foundry unit also confirmed it is producing Rubin's base logic dies on its 4-nanometer process, a dual win analysts say could add 9 trillion won ($6.5 billion) to 2027 semiconductor revenue. Samsung shares closed up 4.1% in Seoul trading.
